# Results: GRU and LSTM against the baselines

160 races from 13 athletes (Strava and Garmin exports). Metrics are on total race time (predicted pace x distance), except `mae_pace_s_km`.

- `chronological`: thesis protocol, the latest 20 % of each athlete's races are held out.
- `grouped-CV`: 5-fold cross-validation holding out whole athletes.

|:--------|:--------------|---------------:|----------------:|-----------:|-----------------:|----------------:|--------:|
| Riegel  | chronological |           7.52 |           19.19 |       8.72 |             4.84 |           26.17 |    0.00 |
| Riegel  | grouped-CV    |           4.54 |           10.82 |       7.31 |             4.89 |           22.16 |    0.00 |

Generated by `race-predictor baselines`; the run's parameters (`params.json`) and raw results (`results.csv`) are saved to its artifacts directory.

`fit_s` is the time of fitting and predicting for the whole protocol: one fit for `chronological`, five for `grouped-CV`. Reproduce with `just sequence` (needs the `deep` extra); one seed, no hyper-parameter tuning.

## Conclusions

- **Neither recurrent model beats Riegel.** On athletes the model has not seen, the median error
  is 9.5 % for the GRU and 12.2 % for the LSTM, against 4.9 % for Riegel, and their MAE is
  2.4 to 2.9 minutes worse (6.9 and 7.4 against 4.5 minutes).
- **On the athletes' next race the LSTM misses less badly, not less often.** It has the lowest
  RMSE of all models (9.1 minutes against 12.7 for ridge and 19.2 for Riegel) and an MAE close to
  ridge (5.3 against 5.0 minutes), so it avoids the large misses on long races that hurt Riegel.
  Its median error is still about twice Riegel's (9.1 % against 4.8 %).
- **GRU and LSTM have no consistent winner.** The LSTM is better on the chronological split,
  the GRU on grouped-CV; with one seed and 160 races the gap is within noise.
- **Why the networks lose.** The sequence models have several thousand weights and 13
  athletes to learn from. Riegel's formula already encodes how fitness scales with distance,
  and the networks have to rediscover it from a few dozen races per fold, with early stopping
  on a validation share of that small set.
- **They are also far more expensive:** 2.5 minutes for the chronological protocol and about
  12 minutes for grouped-CV, against milliseconds for Riegel and ridge.
- **Decision:** the predictor, the command line and the app use Riegel by default, as it is the
  most accurate model on new athletes. The GRU and LSTM stay available (`--model GRU`) for
  comparison once there is more data.
