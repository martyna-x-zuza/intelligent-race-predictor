# Baseline results

160 races from 13 athletes (Strava and Garmin exports). Metrics are on total race time (predicted pace x distance), except `MAE_pace_s_km`.

- `chronological`: thesis protocol, the latest 20 % of each athlete's races are held out.
- `grouped-CV`: 5-fold cross-validation holding out whole athletes.

| model   | protocol      |   MAE_time_min |   RMSE_time_min |   MAPE_% |   MedAPE_% |   MAE_pace_s_km |   fit_s |
|:--------|:--------------|---------------:|----------------:|---------:|-----------:|----------------:|--------:|
| Riegel  | chronological |           7.52 |           19.19 |     8.72 |       4.84 |           26.17 |    0.00 |
| Riegel  | grouped-CV    |           4.54 |           10.82 |     7.31 |       4.89 |           22.16 |    0.01 |
| Ridge   | chronological |           5.02 |           12.65 |     7.23 |       5.04 |           19.54 |    0.01 |
| Ridge   | grouped-CV    |           5.56 |           11.35 |    11.03 |       9.06 |           31.92 |    0.01 |
| HistGB  | chronological |           6.71 |           15.96 |     7.75 |       5.26 |           22.36 |    0.52 |

Generated by `just baselines`; the run's parameters and raw results are saved in `.artifacts/baselines/`.

## Conclusions

- **Riegel is the baseline to beat.** It learns nothing from other athletes, so its typical
  error (median APE about 4.8 %) is the same whether or not the athlete was seen in training.
  The high RMSE in the chronological protocol comes from a few long races, where one
  training run projected over a marathon distance can miss by a lot.
- **Ridge is the best model on athletes it has seen** (chronological: MAPE 7.2 %, MAE 5.0 min),
  but on unseen athletes its typical error almost doubles (median APE 9.1 %). The tabular
  features describe *how much* someone trains much better than *how fast* they are.
- **Gradient boosting overfits.** With 160 races it is worse than ridge in both protocols, and
  the gap is largest on unseen athletes.
- **What this means for the next milestone:** the athlete's own recent runs carry most of the
  signal. Sequence models that read those runs directly (LSTM, GRU) are the next step, with
  Riegel as the reference they have to beat.
